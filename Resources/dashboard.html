        Where it comes from

          Sleep, looked at closely

          Radio signals do change the sleeping brain. Whether that spoils sleep is still open.

          If you sleep better with everything switched off, that is worth taking seriously. Here is what the research can and cannot say about it.

          ✓Established

          The sleeping brain reacts

          • Brain waves change. Half an hour of phone-strength signal at the head before bed alters brain waves in the sleep that follows, mostly the 11–15 Hz “spindle” rhythm. Labs in Zurich and Australia have each found it more than once.
          • It is officially acknowledged. The international body that sets the safety limits calls a small effect on brain waves “the only consistently observed finding”, and says what it means for the body is unclear.
          • People differ. Volunteers who reacted in one study reacted again when retested. A 2025 Zurich study found a 5G signal changed sleep spindles only in people carrying one variant of a gene.
          • It happens inside the safety limit. These experiments used the strength of a phone held to the head, which the limit allows.

          ?Not settled

          Whether sleep gets worse

          • In the lab, results are mixed. In most of those experiments people fell asleep as fast, woke no more often and rated their sleep the same. A Swedish study of 48 people found about ten minutes less deep sleep after three hours of phone-strength signal in the evening.
          • Wi-Fi all night beside the bed (34 men, 2020): sleep unchanged, with one small change in brain waves.
          • A baby monitor in the bedroom for a week (12 adults, 2024): people rated their sleep worse and brain-wave power rose, while measured sleep did not change. The authors say it may be chance and needs a larger trial. Its lead author heads a college that teaches EMF testing.
          • A transmitter switched off. When a Swiss short-wave radio station shut down in 1998, poor sleepers nearby slept better afterwards. It was a different kind of signal, and everyone knew it was off.

          ✕Points the other way

          Belief changes sleep too

          • 397 people, 10 German villages. A test phone mast near their homes was secretly on or off each night. Sleep was the same either way. People worried about masts slept worse even on the nights it was off.
          • 43 people who blamed masts for bad sleep got real or fake shielding over their beds, 465 nights in all. The real shield did no better, and about one in five slept better just believing they were shielded.
          • 119 Swiss bedrooms. Signals were measured in each and sleep tracked by wrist monitor for two weeks. More signal did not mean worse sleep.
          • 14,800 Dutch adults. How much signal people believed they were getting went with sleep problems. How much they actually got did not.

          So your experience fits two explanations, and research cannot yet choose between them for any one person. You may be someone whose sleep reacts to these signals; the brain-wave studies show real differences between people. Or it is everything else that changes when the house goes quiet: no standby lights, no notifications, no screen in the last hour, a deliberate wind-down, and expecting to sleep well. Each of those is known to improve sleep. A blind test at home can tell the two apart.

          Find out for yourself

          A blind test at home

          The only way to know whether it is the signals or the switching-off is to take away your knowledge of which night is which. It takes two weeks and one helper.

          1. Start the trial below and hand the Mac to your helper. The app draws a random plan, half the nights with Wi-Fi off and half with it on, and shows it only to them. They photograph it.
          2. Each evening, once you are in bed, your helper follows the plan: Wi-Fi off on every router, or left on. It must be back on before you get up, so every morning looks the same.
          3. Keep every night the same otherwise. Same bedtime, phone in flight mode and out of reach, router lights out of sight, no looking at router or camera apps.
          4. Score each morning, here, before anything else. How well you slept from 1 to 10, roughly how long it took to fall asleep, and your guess: was it on or off?
          5. After the last night, reveal it together. The app compares your on and off nights and tells you how likely the gap is to be luck.

          ✓No effect found

          Headaches

          In blinded experiments, where people did not know whether a transmitter was switched on, real exposure made no difference to headaches. A 2024 review of those experiments for the World Health Organization found no effect. Headaches at a desk are better explained by screen time, eye strain, posture, dehydration and stress.

          ✓No effect found

          Mind and focus

          Two 2024 reviews for the WHO, one of laboratory experiments and one of long-term studies, found no evidence that radio signals harm memory, attention or thinking. Some measures remain uncertain because the studies were small or disagreed. The brain-wave changes seen in sleep also show up when awake, without any measurable effect on how people think.

          ✓No link in the latest review

          Cancer

          In 2011 the WHO's cancer agency put radio waves in its "possibly carcinogenic" group, the category it uses when evidence is limited. A 2024 review for the WHO of 63 studies then found that mobile phone use likely does not raise the risk of brain tumours, even after ten years or more. Wi-Fi at your seat is far weaker than a phone pressed to your ear.

          ?Real symptoms, not reproduced blind

          Feeling it

          Some people get headaches, tiredness or tingling that they put down to wireless signals, and the symptoms are real. In 46 blinded studies, though, people could not tell when a field was on, and symptoms followed what they believed more than the actual exposure. Critics say those tests were too short and too crude to catch slower effects. If symptoms keep coming back, see a doctor.

          Proven to help sleep and headaches

          • Dim screens and stop scrolling for the last hour before bed, or turn on Night Shift.
          • Silence notifications at night with a Sleep focus, so nothing lights up or buzzes.
          • Keep the bedroom dark: cover or unplug anything with a standby light.
          • Every 20 minutes at a screen, look at something far away for 20 seconds.

          Cutting your exposure at night

          • Set the router to switch its Wi-Fi off overnight, or move it out of the bedroom. Distance counts most: twice as far is a quarter of the signal.
          • Keep your phone off the pillow, and in flight mode if you can.
          • Use the speaker or earphones for long calls. A phone at your ear is the biggest radio exposure of most people's day, and this Mac cannot measure it.
          • These cost nothing. If they help you sleep, keep doing them.

          What it cannot hear

          Mobile networks

          5G, LTE and GSM. A Mac has no cellular radio, so phone-network signal cannot be measured from here. Your phone is the only cellular sensor in the house; its field-test screen shows the real figure. Drawing those bands would need a USB software-defined radio.

          How loud each gadget is

          The scan hears routers, which announce themselves about ten times a second, and Bluetooth devices that advertise. A TV or camera on Wi-Fi only transmits when it has something to send; measuring it needs a packet capture that takes the Mac off Wi-Fi while it listens.

          Everything else

          Microwave ovens, baby monitors, Zigbee and Thread gear, and stray emissions from electronics only show up indirectly, as a higher background-noise figure, and only while this Mac is connected on the 2.4 GHz band, where most of them transmit.

          Readings are what this Mac hears at its current position. Walls and distance change them quickly, and neighbours' devices are counted along with yours. No reading leaves this Mac: the page is served to this computer only.

          While this page is on screen the Mac re-scans the air every few seconds, and each scan makes its own Wi-Fi a little slower for about five seconds. Switch tabs during a call: scanning pauses within ten seconds.
